---
title: Status page
description: A public page that says in one sentence what is wrong and what still works, with 90 days of history and a logbook of incidents.
---

`/status` is the page your customers open when something feels broken. It answers in a sentence: "Payments are failing. The shop and the API work." Below it, one grid shows the last 90 days with a row per component, and a logbook keeps every incident in the words you wrote.

## Choose what it shows

On the **Status page** screen: pick monitors and alerts, name them as customers would, group them, and see the live preview. Or in code, with [`defineStatusPage`](/docs/definitions/status-page), where it is reviewed and deployed like the rest. When the file exists, the screen shows it read-only.

Only what is listed shows. A monitor that goes down marks its component on its own. An alert does the same for anything it watches: an error rate, a stuck queue, failed payments.

## Incidents

Declare an incident on the **Status page** screen with a title, the affected components and its impact (minor, major, critical or maintenance), then post updates as you learn more: investigating, identified, monitoring, resolved. Each update shows on the page at once, in the logbook.

Agents can do it too, with a write token:

```
declare_incident({ title: "Payments failing", impact: "major", components: ["alert:failed-payments"], message: "Card payments are failing. Orders already paid are not affected." })
post_status_update({ incident: 12, state: "resolved", message: "Card payments work again." })
```

Visitors read titles and messages as written, so keep them in plain words. The page is private until you make it public, on the screen or with `public: true` in code.
